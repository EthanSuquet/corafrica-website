Our strategic plan

What we are building next.

Our plan concerns the development of children in rural Nigeria, and the operational capacity and administration to deliver it — all within the setting of the Community Education Centre model.

The priority

John Bosco University.

Our biggest and most urgent undertaking is a university at Ogoja that would not simply grant degrees. It would form teachers, health workers, agricultural leaders, entrepreneurs, peacebuilders and Catholic servant-leaders who do not forget where they came from — young men and women able to rebuild rural communities from within. It is the next step after twenty years of primary and secondary schools.

Capital

What it needs

Approximately US $2 million in foundational support to move the university from vision to reality.

Partnership

What we are looking for

Mission partners, capital support, Catholic academic partnerships, university-development expertise and scholarship donors.

Rationale

Why now

Our secondary schools are producing graduates who have nowhere in the region to go on to. The university closes the last gap in the path we have already built.

Under construction

The academic building at John Bosco Academy, Adagom.

A 56 by 13.5 metre academic block begun in January 2025 with John Stilley and Franciscan University of Steubenville. It houses an assembly hall, two computer laboratories and science laboratories, alongside workshops for fashion design, home economics, beauty and aesthetics, technical drawing, visual arts, music, and photography and videography. Completion is costed at US $129,158.62.

Reach

Who it serves

The academy’s 479 pupils — 343 in the primary section and 136 in the secondary — taught by 28 staff. Four in five are refugee children.

Outcome

What it produces

Around 100 young people a year trained in employable trades, with half the places reserved for women and girls.

The method

Study teams, not just classes.

Through our Vocational and Skills Acquisition Centres, pilot systems are operated in which children and young people form study teams together with parents, teachers and community members. A skill practised alongside the adults who will employ or finance it is a skill that survives leaving school.

Alignment

Where our work meets the global agenda.

CORAfrica’s programmes are aligned to five United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. Our livelihood strategy seeks to improve access to food, healthcare, education, clean water and skills development for vulnerable households, with particular emphasis on rural women.

No poverty

Reducing poverty through education, and through soft loans that let a family build an income.

Zero hunger

Food security through demonstration farms and school feeding.

Gender equality

Increasing rural women’s access to livelihood opportunities, agricultural resources and women-led enterprise.

Decent work

Vocational and skills training that leads to sustainable, dignified livelihoods.

Reduced inequality

Reaching refugee, IDP and host communities together, without distinction.

Partnerships

Working with communities, dioceses, institutions and bilateral organisations rather than around them.
