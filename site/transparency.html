Transparency & accountability

Your gift has an address.

When you give to CORAfrica, your money does not disappear into a system. It becomes something you could drive to and touch: a school bus on a rural road, a fence around a playground, medicine on a clinic shelf, a loan that lets a mother restock her shop.

For twenty years, rural families have trusted us with their children’s futures. You deserve the same honesty from us that they do. So on this page we open our books: what came in, where every naira went, and who checks our work.

Where your gift goes

Almost all of it reaches a child.

Picture a basket of tomatoes carried into a village to be shared. By the time the basket is emptied, almost everything in it has reached the families waiting there. Only a couple of tomatoes were kept back — to pay the driver who made the journey possible.

That is how your gift works with us. In 2025, nearly all the money we spent went straight into classrooms, clinics, farms and family businesses: 92.6%. A small share kept us running — fuel for the vehicles that reach remote communities, phone and internet, stationery, repairs, and the bookkeeping and audit that let us show you this page.

What your money built

Not just figures. Places, people and change.

Education

A school that is safe, and a bus that gets children there

At John Bosco Academy, 2025 was a building year. We invested ₦241.1 million in a new administrative block, fenced the school so children can learn and play in safety, landscaped the grounds, and bought a ₦12.1 million school bus so secondary students can get to class safely every morning. At John Stilley Schools, we built accommodation for the National Youth Service Corps members posted to the school, began fencing the compound and improved the grounds. In total, education received ₦310.4 million.

Economic empowerment

A loan that keeps a child in school

A child stays in school when the family at home can pay for it. That is why we lent ₦29.7 million, interest-free, to small businesses in the communities we serve, and gave ₦13.5 million in direct support to widows and the most vulnerable households. No interest. No exploitation. Just a fair start.

Healthcare

Medicine within reach

A sick child cannot learn. In 2025 we took a medical outreach to Victoria, stocked our medical centre with ₦11.9 million worth of drugs and supplies, and kept its doors open to the community. Total health investment: ₦27.0 million.

Agriculture

Farms that feed and teach

Our farms produce food and teach practical skills. In 2025 we invested ₦20.5 million in raising livestock, growing crops and running the farms.

The honest part

In 2025, we spent more than we received. Here is why.

We received ₦383.1 million and spent ₦433.1 million. The ₦50.1 million difference (about US $31,800) came from savings we carried into the year. Rather than leave that money sitting in the bank, we used it to finish work that children needed now — above all, the new administrative block and safer school compounds.

It means we begin 2026 with lean savings. We are telling you because you have a right to know, and because it is exactly why your support matters now.

Audited accounts

Don’t take our word for it.

Every year, our accounts are independently audited by Akomaye Adie & Co., Chartered Accountants & Tax Practitioners, Calabar. In each of the last four years the auditors have confirmed that our financial statements give a true and fair view of CORAfrica’s affairs.

The full audited statements for 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 are available to funders on request.
